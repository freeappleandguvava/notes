## Osmosis
Diffusion of water across a semi-permiable membrane.
- water moves from an areaof higher water (lower solute) concentraion to one of (higher solute) concentration.

![[../../../__support/Types of Transport-6.png|413]]

### Dynamic Equilibrium
when the concentration of solution is equal on bot sides, the water still moves from one side to another, but there is no net movement. *This state is called Dynamic Equilibrium*,
- there is no gradient present during dynamic equilibrium.

#### Types of Solutions
- **Hypertonic** - when solute concentration of one side is higher than the other side.
- **Isotonic** - when solute concentration of both sides are equal
- **Hypertonic** - when solute concetration of one side is lower to the other side. w

###### What if you place a cell in Hypertonic Solution?
- than the water will move into the cell, in this case the cell will fill with water and become turgid (inflate).
- sometimes the difference is so much that the cell can burst, cell bursting is called **Cytolysis** or *Hemolysis* specifically for RBC.
- Plant cells do not burst, the cell wall in plant cells prevent cells from bursting.


###### What if you place a cell in Hypotonic Solution?
- The water will move out of the cell.
- The will deflate , this is called becoming **Flaccid**.
- When animal cell becomes flaccid, it's called **Crenation**
- When plant cell becomes flaccid, it's called **plasmolysis**.

###### What if you place a cell in Isotonic Solution?
- The cell will be unaffected
- The movement of water will be equal on both in/out.
- (*Dynamic Equilibrium*)

## Primary Transport
### Sodium - Potassium Pump
- The $Na+/K+$ pump moves $3Na+$ out for every $2K+$ in.
- Uses hydrolysis to break down $ATP$ into $ADP + Pi$
- Sodium moves from low concentration inside the cell to high concentration outside of the cell & Potassium moves form low concentraion outside the cell to high concentration inside the cell.
- Both $Na+$ & $K+$ are moving against/up the gradient.

![NaKpump|553](https://i.makeagif.com/media/12-16-2025/H7R5km.gif)

### Electrochemical Gradient
- Accumulation of more positive/negative ions outside/inside of the cell.
- because there are more ions outside of the cell than inside, this diff in changes creates a electrochemical gradient.
- In living systems gradients are more complex
- because atoms and molecules can form ions and carry +/- charges, there also may be electrical gradient (diff in charge)
- More positive ions outside of the cell compared to the inside of the cell causes the outside to become more positive and inside to become negative creating **Membrane potential**
- **Membrane Potential** is essentially an electrical potential difference or voltage across the cell membrane.
- Membrane potential is very very important for neurons

There fore electrochemical Gradient is the combined gradient of a concentration difference and electrical charge that affects an ion.


## Secondary Active Transport
**Cotransport** transporting to same side
- The electrochemical Gradient can now provide energy for moving other substances against their own concentration gradient, this is called **Secondary Active Transport**
- The movement of ions down an electrochemical gradient is coupled to the transport of other substances against their concentration gradient.
- *Ions move down the gradient* while, *Nutrient Molecules* use that energy loss to *move against the gradient*
Example: Sodium-Glucose transporter. (cotransport)

- Inward movement of sodium ions is coupled with the outer movement of another substance, in this example Calcium ions.
- Sodium moves against the gradient while, Calcium moves down the gradient.

3. **Receptor Mediated Endocytosis** : Selectively brings a specific substance into the cell using membrane receptors.
