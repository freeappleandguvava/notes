## What is plasma membrane made of
- Made of phospholipid bilayer, with embedded cholesterol(for fluidity) and proteins (for transport etc.)

### Phospholipid bilayer
- **Hydrophilic** polar head (the phosphate group)
- two **hydrophobic** non-polar fatty acid tails

### Cholestrol
cholestrol *fulidity buffer* and *temperature buffer*

**Structure of Cholesterol** : Cholesterol has $-OH$ head, which forms hydorgen bond with the oxygen of phospate group in the phospholipid. Cholesterol also has a hydrophoic non-polar tail and body, which mimics phospholipds perfectly. 

Cholesterol can be bidirectional.

**At High Temperature**
- At high temperatures, lipids naturally gain kinetic energy and vibrate rapidly. This causes phospholipids to spread apart, threatening to make the membrane **too fluid, structurally unstable, and leaky**

	How does cholesterol Help?
	- The rigid, flat carbon rings of cholesterol act like molecular "clamps." They pack tightly against the vibrating fatty acid tails, using strong Van der Waals attractions to brace them. By restricting this wild movement, cholesterol **holds the phospholipids together, lowers fluidity, and prevents the membrane from disintegrating**

**At Cold Temperature**
- At low temperatures, lipids lose their kinetic energy. Left on their own, the fatty acid tails would stop moving, pack tightly against one another, and crystallize into a **rigid, frozen gel** that shatters easily.

	How does Cholesterol Help?
	- Because cholesterol has an irregular, bulky shape, it acts as a molecular "wedge." It inserts itself directly between the phospholipids, physically blocking their fatty acid tails from coming too close together and locking into a crystal lattice. By disrupting this tight packing, cholesterol **maintains a baseline level of fluidity and prevents the membrane from freezing**

## The Fluid Mosaic Model
desvribes membrane as both a fluid and a mosaic.

**Fluid** - Components are in constant movment. The structure of phospholipids and presence of cholesterol keeps the membrane fluid. (all the components are free to move, every phospholipid is always moving laterally or flip=flop,)  *basically eveyrthing is in motions*

**Mosaic** - Plasma membrane is mosaic because it has many different types of molecules embedded throughout.


### Temperature Change
Ratio of saturated vs unsaturated lipids is what prevents cells from freezing. At low temperature bent from double Carbon bonds of unsaturated fatty acids disables cells to pack closely together preventing rupture from decreased fluidity.

Organisms like fish adapts to cold temperatue by increasing amount of unsaturated fatty acids in the membrane.
that's why fish are good source of health unsaturatted fatty acids.


# Uses of Fluid Mosaic Model
1. **Structural support** -  by attachin to the cytoskeleton
2. **Recognition** - Glycoproteins (proteins carrying sugar molecules) provide cell with a unique identity and are specific to each individual. They also help immune system identify foregin cell in your body such as organ transplants.
3. **Communication** - protuding proteins can act as receptor sites for hormones.
4. **Enzymatic Activity** - some membrane proteins, such as those associated with respirations and photosynthesis are enzymes.
5. **Transport** - Can serve as channels for molecules that are too large or too hydrophilic to pass through the phospholipid bilayer.
