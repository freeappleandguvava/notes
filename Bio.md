## Endotherms vs Ectotherms

**Endotherms** (warm blooded) organisms that generate and maintain their body heat internally through metabolic processes, they maintain a stable internal temperature regardless of external fluctuations.

**Ectotherms** (cold blooded) Organisms that rely primarily on external environmental sources (like sunlight or heated surfaces) to regulate their body temperatire. Their internal emperature shifts according to their surroundings.

##### Examples:
**Endotherms**: Mammals, Birds, Lamnid sharks.
**Ectotherms**: Reptiles, Amphibians, Fish, Invertebrates.


#### How does it work? 
###### Ectotherms: 
- _When it get's Cold_ The external temperature drop cools the animal's tissues. Biochemical reactions and enzyme activites slow down. As a result the rate of ccellular respiration drops significantly , requiring less food and causing the animals to  become sluggish.
- *When it gets warmer* Kinertic energy increase, causing enzymes to process substances faster. Respiration rates climb exponentially until a critical thermal maximum is reached, which can damage internal proteins.
###### Endotherms: 
- *Within the Thermoneutral Zone:* This is the range of environmental temperatures where the animal's body doesn't need to expend extra energy to stay comfortable. Respiration remains baseline and stable.
- *When it get colder* Below the thermoneutral zone, the animal loses body heat to the environment. To compensate, it must generate internal heat through physical methods like shivering or chemical metabolic acceleration. This dramatic metabolic ramp-up **increases cellular respiration rates** to produce cellular energy (ATP) and byproduct heat.
- *When it gets hotter* Above the thermoneutral zone, respiration rates **increase slightly** again. This covers the biological energy required for physiological cooling mechanisms like panting, sweating, and rapid blood pumping.
